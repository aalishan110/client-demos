Nurses in Germany

Germany recruits foreign nurses for hospitals and care homes. The route usually combines three things: recognition of your nursing qualification, German language at B2 level, and a work or recognition visa. This guide sets out which comes first, which comes next, and where people get stuck.

1

Recognition

Your Bangladeshi nursing qualification is compared with German nursing training by the competent authority in the state where you plan to work. The result is full recognition, or a requirement to complete an adaptation course or a knowledge test first.

You’ll need

  • Nursing diploma or BSc certificate and transcripts
  • Registration with the Bangladesh Nursing and Midwifery Council
  • Certified translations of every document
  • Proof of work experience

Watch out

Authorities differ by state and so do processing times. Apply in the state where you expect to work, and keep names and dates identical across every document.

2

German language

German is the real gate. Professional recognition generally requires B2, and many states also ask for a healthcare-specific language test. Starting at B1 and building steadily is the common path.

You’ll need

  • Enrolment in a structured German course
  • A recognised exam certificate (for example Goethe or telc)
  • Practice with clinical vocabulary and patient conversation

Watch out

Short “fast-track” courses rarely prepare you for real conversations with patients. Budget genuine study time.

3

Employer and contract

Most nurses secure an employer before they travel, and the employer often supports the recognition process. Compare offers rather than accepting the first.

You’ll need

  • A written contract
  • A clear statement of pay band and working hours
  • Who pays for language courses and recognition
  • Accommodation arrangements

Watch out

Be wary of any placement fee charged to you before a verifiable contract exists.

4

Visa

With recognition (or a recognition pathway) and a contract in hand, you apply at the German mission for the appropriate visa.

You’ll need

  • Valid passport
  • The signed contract
  • Recognition decision or notice
  • Proof of language level
  • Completed forms and insurance

Watch out

Appointment waiting times at the mission can be long. Build them into your plan.

5

Arrival and registration

After arrival you complete the remaining recognition steps and register locally. The first months are an adaptation period.

You’ll need

  • Address registration
  • Health insurance
  • Any outstanding recognition requirement
  • A local bank account

Watch out

Plan your finances for the first months. Income may start slowly while paperwork completes.

Realistic expectations

Recognition decisions and timelines depend on the state authority and on your documents. We plan with ranges and risks, not promises.
