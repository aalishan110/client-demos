Engineers in Germany

Engineers usually enter Germany through the EU Blue Card or a skilled-worker route. Which one applies depends on your degree, how it is recognised, and the salary threshold set for the year.

1

Degree status

Confirm how your university and degree are rated in Germany’s recognition database, and whether your profession is regulated.

You’ll need

  • Degree certificate and transcripts
  • Official translations
  • Your university’s status in the German database

Watch out

Not every degree is treated alike. Check before you apply for roles that assume recognition.

2

Job offer and threshold

A job offer must meet the salary requirement for the route you choose. The figure changes, so we confirm the current one.

You’ll need

  • A written job offer
  • A contract that states gross salary clearly
  • Employer registration details

Watch out

An offer below the threshold can close a route that looked open.

3

Language

Requirements depend on the employer and the role. Many technical teams work in English, but daily life and some regulated roles need German.

You’ll need

  • A clear statement from the employer about working language
  • A German study plan if you want to settle long term

Watch out

Do not assume English is enough for every role or every city.

4

Visa

Prepare the contract, degree evidence and passport documents together, and apply at the mission.

You’ll need

  • Valid passport
  • Contract and degree evidence
  • Health insurance proof
  • Completed forms

Watch out

Incomplete document sets are the most common reason for delay.

Realistic expectations

Thresholds and rules are updated regularly. We confirm the current figures before you rely on them.
