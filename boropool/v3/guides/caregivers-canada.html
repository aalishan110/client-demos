Caregivers in Canada

Canada’s caregiver immigration programmes have changed several times in recent years. Before you spend anything, the first job is to confirm which programme is open today and whether your profile fits it.

1

Confirm the programme

Rules, caps and eligibility change. Before you spend anything, confirm which programme is open today and whether your profile fits it.

You’ll need

  • The current official programme criteria
  • A summary of your education and work history
  • Your plan for language testing

Watch out

Anyone quoting a fixed route based on last year’s rules is guessing.

2

Language evidence

Accepted English or French test results are required, and your score decides which options are realistic.

You’ll need

  • A booking for an accepted test (for English, for example IELTS General or CELPIP)
  • Practice materials and mock tests
  • Results that are still valid when you apply

Watch out

Test results expire. Time your test close to your application.

3

Education and experience

Certificates, training records and verifiable work history are gathered early. Gaps and inconsistencies slow a file down.

You’ll need

  • Certificates and transcripts
  • Reference letters on employer letterhead
  • Proof of paid caregiving experience where required

Watch out

Informal experience that cannot be documented may not count.

4

Job offer and LMIA

Some routes need an employer offer, and some of those need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) arranged by the employer.

You’ll need

  • A written offer
  • Employer details you can verify
  • Confirmation of who handles the LMIA

Watch out

A genuine employer does not charge you for a job offer or an LMIA. Never pay for a “guaranteed” offer.

5

Apply and settle

A complete application, then practical planning for arrival, housing and the first months.

You’ll need

  • A complete application
  • Police and medical requirements
  • Funds and an arrival plan

Watch out

Incomplete files are returned or delayed. Checking before submission can save months.

Realistic expectations

Never pay for a “guaranteed” job offer. A genuine employer does not charge you for it, and any fee should be explained and documented.
